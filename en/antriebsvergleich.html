Assumptions & Formulas

We calculate using typical energy contents (lower heating value, rounded averages): - Gasoline Super (E5/E10/Super Plus): approx. 8.9–9.0 kWh per liter - Diesel: approx. 9.8 kWh per liter Formulas: 1) Gasoline/diesel energy consumption: kWh/100 km = Consumption (L/100 km) × kWh/L 2) Gasoline/diesel cost: €/100 km = Consumption (L/100 km) × Fuel price (€/L) 3) Electric cost: €/100 km = Consumption (kWh/100 km) × Electricity price (€/kWh)

The actual energy content of fuels varies slightly depending on composition (gasoline approx. 8.7–9.1 kWh/L, diesel approx. 9.7–9.9 kWh/L). The averages used here serve as a rough comparison and do not replace exact manufacturer figures. Charging losses for electric cars (typically a few percent) are not shown separately in the electricity cost calculation.

Sources: Umweltbundesamt – Publications on fuels & emissions, ADAC – Electric vehicle guide, Wikipedia – Electric car

Notes on the Powertrain Comparison

With this calculator you can directly compare the energy consumption and pure energy costs of gasoline/diesel vehicles and electric cars. Enter the consumption in L/100 km and kWh/100 km along with current fuel and electricity prices.

This lets you see at a glance how switching from gasoline or diesel to an electric car would affect your individual situation. Especially with fluctuating energy prices, a transparent comparison per 100 km helps you better evaluate purchase decisions.

In Practice: Gasoline/Diesel or Electric – What Matters

Whether an electric car pays off financially depends heavily on your own electricity price (home wallbox vs. expensive public fast chargers) and the local fuel price – this calculator makes the pure energy cost comparison transparent.

Anyone who charges mostly at home with cheap off-peak electricity or their own solar system ends up with significantly lower costs per 100 km than when charging at public fast chargers.

For a complete cost calculation (total cost of ownership), purchase price, depreciation, maintenance, and insurance should be considered alongside energy costs – this calculator deliberately covers only the energy cost per 100 km.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an electric car cheaper to run than a gasoline or diesel car?

When it comes to pure energy costs per 100 km, an electric car charged at home is usually significantly cheaper than a comparable gasoline or diesel car. However, if you mostly rely on expensive public fast chargers, that advantage can shrink considerably.

How many kWh does an electric car use per 100 km?

Most electric cars use between 15 and 22 kWh/100 km, depending on vehicle size, driving style, outside temperature, and speed.

Why does an EV use more energy in winter?

Cold reduces battery efficiency, and cabin heating requires additional energy, which can noticeably increase consumption per 100 km in winter.

How many kWh of energy does 1 liter of gasoline contain?

One liter of gasoline contains roughly 8.9–9.0 kWh of energy, and one liter of diesel about 9.8 kWh – considerably more than an electric car needs in electrical energy for the same distance, though the combustion engine has lower efficiency.

Are charging losses for the electric car included in this calculator?

No, this calculator compares the pure energy costs based on the stated consumption. Charging losses (typically a few percent) are not shown separately and would slightly increase real-world costs.
