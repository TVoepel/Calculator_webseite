Formula & Sources

EV (relative to ISO 100) = log₂(aperture number² ÷ shutter speed ÷ (ISO ÷ 100)) Equivalent exposure (same EV): New time = Old time × (New aperture² ÷ Old aperture²) × (Old ISO ÷ New ISO)

The classical f-stop series follows steps of √2: f/1.0 – 1.4 – 2 – 2.8 – 4 – 5.6 – 8 – 11 – 16 – 22 – 32. Each stop halves or doubles the amount of light transmitted.

Sources: Wikipedia – Exposure value (EV), Wikipedia – f-number

In Practice

When switching from a lens with f/2.8 to one with a maximum of f/5.6 (2 stops darker), you need to quadruple the shutter speed or quadruple the ISO to get the same exposure.

For long exposures with ND filters (e.g. 6 stops), equivalent exposure helps convert from a normal shutter speed to the much longer time needed with the filter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is exposure value (EV)?

Exposure value combines aperture and shutter speed into a single number that describes the amount of light in a shot. EV 15 corresponds to a sunny day, for example, while EV 5 corresponds to a dark indoor room.

How much more light does f/2.8 let in compared to f/5.6?

At the same shutter speed, f/2.8 lets in about 4× as much light as f/5.6 (a difference of 2 stops; each stop doubles or halves the amount of light).

How are ISO 100 and ISO 1600 related?

ISO 1600 is 4 stops (2⁴ = 16×) more sensitive than ISO 100, so it needs only a sixteenth of the light for the same exposure – though usually with noticeably more image noise.

What is equivalent exposure?

Two combinations of aperture, shutter speed, and ISO are equivalent when they let the same amount of light reach the sensor (the same EV). If the aperture is closed down (more depth of field), the shutter speed must be lengthened or the ISO increased to achieve the same exposure.

What is the exposure triangle?

Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO together form the exposure triangle: changing any one value requires adjusting the others to keep the same exposure – while each value also affects depth of field (aperture), motion blur (shutter speed), and image noise (ISO).
