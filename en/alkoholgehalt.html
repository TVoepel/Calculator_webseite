Formula & Sources

Vol% (ABV) = Alcohol volume ÷ Total volume × 100 US Proof = Vol% × 2 UK Proof (historical, before 1980) = Vol% × 1.75 Pure alcohol amount: mL of alcohol = Beverage volume [mL] × Vol% ÷ 100 Grams of alcohol = mL of alcohol × 0.789 (ethanol density at 20 °C)

A common rule of thumb in home brewing/distilling is ABV ≈ (original gravity − final gravity) × 131.25 – this is a simplified approximation commonly used in practice, not an exact physical formula.

In Practice

US spirits bottles often also state alcohol content in Proof – the conversion helps compare it with European Vol% figures.

Cocktail recipes can be converted to the actual alcohol content of the finished drink this way, e.g. 50 mL of rum (40%) in a 200 mL cocktail results in 10% Vol in the total drink.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Vol% and Proof?

Vol% states the alcohol content directly (e.g. 40% vodka = 40 mL of alcohol per 100 mL). US Proof is double the Vol% value (40% = 80 Proof); UK Proof (historical) equals Vol% × 1.75.

How many grams of pure alcohol are in a beer?

500 mL of beer at 5% Vol contains 500 × 0.05 = 25 mL of pure alcohol. With an ethanol density of about 0.789 g/mL, that's roughly 19.7 g of pure alcohol.

How do you dilute alcohol to a lower concentration?

Using the dilution formula c₁ × V₁ = c₂ × V₂. Example: 1 L of 50% spirits mixed with 0.25 L of water yields 1.25 L at 40% Vol.

How is alcohol content measured in home distilling?

With an alcoholmeter (hydrometer) directly in the distillate, or by calculation from the density before and after fermentation. A common rule of thumb is ABV ≈ (original gravity − final gravity) × 131.25 – a simplified approximation, not an exact physical formula.

Why do US Proof and UK Proof differ?

The US has defined Proof as exactly double the Vol% value since 1848. The historical British Proof system (before 1980) was based on a gunpowder test method and resulted in the factor 1.75 – since 1980, the UK has also used only Vol%.
