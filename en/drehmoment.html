Formula & Reference Values

Torque M = force F × lever arm r, base unit of this calculator = 1 newton-meter (Nm) 1 kgf·m = 9.80665 Nm (from standard gravity g = 9.80665 m/s²) 1 lbf·ft = 1.3558179483 Nm (from 1 lbf = 4.4482216153 N, 1 ft = 0.3048 m) 1 lbf·in = 0.1129848290 Nm 1 oz·in = 0.0070615518 Nm Power: P [kW] = M [Nm] × n [1/min] ÷ 9550

Sources: BIPM – SI Brochure, NIST – Guide to Unit Conversion, Wikipedia – Torque

In Practice

When changing a wheel or tightening bolts, the manufacturer specifies a tightening torque in Nm – a torque wrench ensures bolts are neither too loose nor overtightened.

US tools and engine datasheets often give torque in lbf·ft (frequently abbreviated as "ft-lbs") – for international vehicle comparisons, converting to Nm is helpful.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is torque?

Torque is the product of force and lever arm (M = F × r). A force of 10 N at a lever arm of 1 m gives a torque of 10 Nm.

What is the difference between torque and power?

Torque (Nm) describes the force effect, power (kW/hp) is torque times rotational speed. More Nm means more force/acceleration, more kW means more power delivered across the rev range – see our Motor Comparison calculator.

How do you convert Nm to lbf·ft?

1 Nm equals 0.737562 lbf·ft, and 1 lbf·ft equals 1.355818 Nm – derived from 1 lbf = 4.4482216 N and 1 ft = 0.3048 m.

How do you calculate power from torque and RPM?

P [kW] = M [Nm] × n [1/min] ÷ 9550. Example: 300 Nm at 4000 RPM gives about 125.6 kW (approx. 171 hp).

What tightening torque do wheel bolts need?

Typical values for steel rims are around 110 Nm, for alloy wheels often 120–130 Nm – but the manufacturer's specification in the vehicle manual is always authoritative.
