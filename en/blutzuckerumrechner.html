Convert Blood Glucose: mg/dl ↔ mmol/l

In Germany, Austria, the US, and some other countries, blood glucose is usually measured in mg/dl, while the SI unit system (and much of Europe) uses mmol/l. This calculator converts glucose values in both directions.

Formula

mmol/l = mg/dl ÷ 18.0156 mg/dl = mmol/l × 18.0156 (often rounded as: mg/dl × 0.0555 = mmol/l, or mmol/l × 18.02 = mg/dl)

The factor 18.0156 comes from the molar mass of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆ = 180.156 g/mol): 1 mmol/l of glucose corresponds to 180.156 mg/l = 18.0156 mg/dl. Our general concentration converter uses this same formula (mass = molarity × molar mass).

Reference Values (Guideline Figures)

Diagnostic thresholds based on common clinical guidelines (WHO / ADA / German Diabetes Association), as summarized among others by diabetesDE:

CategoryFasting Blood Glucose
Normal< 100 mg/dl (< 5.6 mmol/l)
Prediabetes (impaired fasting glucose)100–125 mg/dl (5.6–6.9 mmol/l)
Diabetes mellitus (confirmed on 2 days, or + HbA1c/OGTT)≥ 126 mg/dl (≥ 7.0 mmol/l)
Normal, 2 hrs after eating (postprandial)< 140 mg/dl (< 7.8 mmol/l)
⚠️ These values are for general information only and do not replace a medical diagnosis or consultation. A single measurement is not sufficient for a diabetes diagnosis. If you have questions about your personal blood glucose values, please consult your physician or diabetes care team.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many mmol/l is 100 mg/dl of blood glucose?

100 mg/dl is roughly equal to 5.55 mmol/l (100 ÷ 18.0156).

How many mg/dl is 7.0 mmol/l of blood glucose?

7.0 mmol/l is roughly equal to 126.1 mg/dl (7.0 × 18.0156) – a commonly cited diabetes threshold for fasting blood glucose.

Why is blood glucose measured in mg/dl in some countries and mmol/l in others?

Both units became established regionally for historical reasons: mmol/l is the SI-compliant unit and the standard in most countries, while mg/dl is used in, among others, the US, parts of Germany, Austria, France, and Japan.

At what level is blood glucose considered diabetes?

A fasting blood glucose level of 126 mg/dl (7.0 mmol/l) or above, confirmed on a second day or with an additional test, is considered indicative of diabetes mellitus under common clinical guidelines. The final diagnosis is always made by a physician.

Can I change the unit on my blood glucose meter?

Many meters and apps (e.g. FreeStyle Libre, Apple Health) let you switch between mg/dl and mmol/l in the device settings – this calculator still helps you quickly compare existing values.
