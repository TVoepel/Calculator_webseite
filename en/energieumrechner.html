All Supported Energy Units

With this energy converter, you can directly convert joules, kilojoules, watt-hours, kilowatt-hours, calories, kilocalories, electronvolts, BTU, and foot-pounds.

Kilowatt-hours (kWh) are the standard billing unit for electricity in Germany, while BTU is commonly used for air conditioning and heating capacity in English-speaking countries.

In Practice: When You Need to Convert Energy Units

Food packaging usually lists nutritional values in both kilojoules (kJ) and kilocalories (kcal) – if you plan your calorie needs in kcal, you need to convert the kJ figure.

Electricity bills are calculated in kilowatt-hours (kWh); comparing the energy consumption of devices in joules or watt-hours requires converting between units.

Air conditioners from the US market state their cooling capacity in BTU rather than kW – important for choosing the right unit size for a room.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many kJ is 1 kcal?

1 kilocalorie (kcal) equals 4.184 kilojoules (kJ). Nutrition labels often list both values side by side.

How many joules is 1 kWh?

1 kilowatt-hour equals 3,600,000 joules (3.6 megajoules) – the energy delivered by a power of 1 kW over one hour.

What does BTU mean for air conditioners?

BTU (British Thermal Unit) is an energy unit commonly used in English-speaking countries. 1 BTU equals about 1055 joules; the cooling capacity of air conditioners is often given in BTU/h.

What's the difference between a calorie and a kilocalorie?

In everyday language, "calorie" on food labels usually means kilocalorie (kcal). Physically, however, 1 kcal equals 1000 (small) calories – a common source of confusion in nutrition labeling.

How many joules is 1 electronvolt?

1 electronvolt (eV) equals exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules – an exact value since the 2019 SI redefinition, commonly used in particle and atomic physics.
