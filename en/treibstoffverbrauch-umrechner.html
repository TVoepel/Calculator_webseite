Formula & Reference Values

These figures are reciprocal to one another, so there is no fixed conversion factor: km/L = 100 ÷ (L/100 km) mi/L = km/L ÷ 1.609344 mpg (US) = 235.214583 ÷ (L/100 km) mpg (UK) = 282.480936 ÷ (L/100 km) Derivation mpg (US): 100 km ÷ 1.609344 km/mi = miles per (L/100km) liters; 1 US gallon = 3.785411784 L ⇒ constant = 100 × 3.785411784 ÷ 1.609344 ≈ 235.214583 Derivation mpg (UK): 1 imperial gallon = 4.54609 L ⇒ constant = 100 × 4.54609 ÷ 1.609344 ≈ 282.480936

A lower L/100km figure means less fuel consumed, and a higher mpg figure also means less fuel consumed (mpg counts distance traveled per unit of fuel) – so the two scales run in opposite directions.

All Supported Consumption Units

With this fuel consumption converter you can directly convert liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km), kilometers per liter (km/L), miles per liter (mi/L), miles per US gallon (mpg US), and miles per UK gallon (mpg UK).

L/100 km is the consumption figure commonly used in Germany and the EU, while mpg is used in the US and the UK – though US and UK gallons differ in size (3.785 L vs. 4.546 L).

In Practice: When You Need to Convert Fuel Consumption

When importing or comparing US vehicles, consumption is often given in mpg (miles per gallon) instead of L/100 km – a direct comparison is barely possible without converting.

Because US and UK gallons differ in size, mpg (US) also differs noticeably from mpg (UK) for the same vehicle.

For fleet comparisons or cost estimates on long trips, converting helps make consumption figures from different countries comparable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert mpg to L/100 km?

For US gallons: L/100 km = 235.214583 ÷ mpg. A vehicle with 30 mpg (US) therefore uses about 7.84 L/100 km.

Why do mpg (US) and mpg (UK) differ?

The US gallon (3.785 L) is smaller than the imperial gallon (4.546 L). At the same consumption, the UK gallon therefore gives a higher mpg figure than the US gallon.

What counts as good fuel consumption in L/100 km?

Small cars often use 5–6 L/100 km, mid-size cars 6–8 L/100 km, and SUVs frequently more than 8 L/100 km – but real-world figures depend heavily on driving style and route.

What does WLTP mean for consumption figures?

WLTP (Worldwide Harmonized Light Vehicles Test Procedure) has been the mandatory EU test procedure for determining consumption and emissions figures since 2018/2021, replacing the older NEDC procedure.

How do you calculate km/L from L/100 km?

Formula: km/L = 100 ÷ (L/100 km). At a consumption of 6.5 L/100 km, that works out to about 15.4 km/L.
