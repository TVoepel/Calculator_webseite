All Supported Speed Units

With this speed converter, you can directly convert meters per second (m/s), kilometers per hour (km/h), miles per hour (mph), knots (kn), feet per second (ft/s), and Mach.

km/h is used for road traffic in Europe, mph in English-speaking countries, and knots in shipping and aviation.

In Practice: When You Need to Convert Speeds

US speed limits are given in mph (miles per hour) – important for anyone driving in the US or comparing vehicle data from the US.

In shipping and aviation, speed is traditionally measured in knots (nautical miles per hour), while wind speeds are given in km/h, m/s, or knots depending on the source.

Bike computers and sports watches from the US market also sometimes display mph instead of km/h.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert mph to km/h?

1 mph (mile per hour) equals 1.609344 km/h. 60 mph is therefore about 96.6 km/h.

How fast is 1 knot in km/h?

1 knot equals 1 nautical mile per hour, or 1.852 km/h.

Why is speed at sea measured in knots?

The knot originates historically from a measuring method using a knotted line, and it has remained a practical unit in shipping and aviation to this day because it ties directly into nautical miles and nautical charts.

How fast is Mach 1?

Mach 1 corresponds to the speed of sound and depends on altitude and air temperature. Under standard conditions at sea level (15 °C), it's about 340.3 m/s, or 1225 km/h.

How does the German speed limit of 130 km/h compare to US highways?

130 km/h is about 80.8 mph. Many US highways allow 65–75 mph (roughly 105–121 km/h), so their limits are lower than Germany's recommended speed.
