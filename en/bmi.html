Formulas & Sources

BMI = weight [kg] ÷ height [m]² Mifflin-St Jeor (1990), basal metabolic rate in kcal/day: Men: BMR = 10 × weight[kg] + 6.25 × height[cm] − 5 × age + 5 Women: BMR = 10 × weight[kg] + 6.25 × height[cm] − 5 × age − 161 Harris-Benedict, revised 1984 (for comparison): Men: BMR = 88.362 + 13.397 × weight + 4.799 × height − 5.677 × age Women: BMR = 447.593 + 9.247 × weight + 3.098 × height − 4.330 × age TDEE = BMR × activity factor (1.2 to 1.9)

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is now generally considered more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula in nutrition science and is shown here as the primary result.

In Practice

For realistic weight loss, a calorie deficit of about 500 kcal/day is a common rule of thumb for around 0.5 kg of weight loss per week – your TDEE is the starting point from which the deficit is subtracted.

Strength and endurance athletes often use TDEE to plan the right calorie intake for muscle building (slight surplus) or cutting phases (deficit).

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my BMI?

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Example: 75 kg ÷ (1.75 m)² = 24.5 (normal weight according to the WHO classification).

What are the BMI categories according to the WHO?

Below 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 normal weight, 25–29.9 overweight (pre-obesity), 30–34.9 obesity class I, 35–39.9 obesity class II, 40 and above obesity class III.

What is basal metabolic rate (BMR)?

Basal metabolic rate is the amount of energy the body needs for vital functions while completely at rest. The Mifflin-St Jeor formula (1990) is now generally considered more accurate than the older Harris-Benedict formula.

How do I calculate my daily calorie needs (TDEE)?

TDEE = basal metabolic rate (BMR) × activity factor. The factor ranges from 1.2 (little to no exercise) to 1.9 (very hard physical work plus training).

Is BMI a reliable health indicator?

BMI does not account for muscle mass, body composition, or fat distribution, and can be misleading for very muscular or very lean individuals. It serves as a rough guideline, not a medical diagnosis.
