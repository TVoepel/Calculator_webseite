Formulas & Notes on Syntax

Mass concentration [g/L] = Molar concentration [mol/L] × Molar mass [g/mol] Molar concentration [mol/L] = Mass concentration [g/L] ÷ Molar mass [g/mol] Molality ↔ Molarity (also requires solution density ρ in kg/L): Molarity c [mol/L] = Molality b [mol/kg] × ρ ÷ (1 + b × Molar mass ÷ 1000) Molality b [mol/kg] = Molarity c [mol/L] ÷ (ρ − c × Molar mass ÷ 1000) Formula syntax: - Element symbols capitalized, optional lowercase 2nd letter (Na, Cl, Fe, Ca) - Parentheses ( ) and [ ] with a multiplier, e.g. Ca3(PO4)2, Fe2(SO4)3 - Hydrates with · or *, e.g. MgSO4·7H2O, CuSO4*5H2O - Charges optional with ^, e.g. SO4^2-, NH4^+ (charge does not change the molar mass)

For ppm/ppb, the approximation commonly used for dilute aqueous solutions is 1 ppm ≈ 1 mg/L (solution density ≈ 1 kg/L). This approximation does not hold for highly concentrated solutions, other solvents, or gas mixtures (ppm by volume).

Sanity check: for creatinine (M = 113.12 g/mol), this gives the conversion factor well known in clinical chemistry, µmol/L = mg/dL × 88.4 – which matches this calculator exactly.

Molality refers to the mass of the solvent (mol/kg), while molarity refers to the volume of the solution (mol/L) – which is why converting between them additionally requires the solution density. For dilute aqueous solutions, ρ ≈ 1.00 kg/L is a common approximation; for more concentrated solutions, the actual density deviates noticeably from this.

All Supported Concentration Units

Molar units: mol/L, mmol/L, µmol/L, nmol/L. Mass units: g/L, mg/L, µg/L, mg/dL, mg/mL, µg/mL, ppm, ppb. Molal units: mol/kg, mmol/kg (also requires the solution density).

Built-in compounds: nitrate, nitrite, sulfate, phosphate, ammonium, glutamate, glutamine, creatinine, creatine. All other substances can be entered as a chemical formula (e.g. NaCl, C6H12O6, CaCO3) or directly as a molar mass value.

In Practice: When You Need to Convert Concentrations

In aquariums, nitrate, nitrite, phosphate, and ammonium levels are usually measured in mg/L – but scientific limit values or lab analyses often give the same values in mmol/L.

Lab results (e.g. electrolytes, creatinine) are reported in either mg/dL or mmol/L or µmol/L, depending on the country and lab, which makes comparing values harder.

In chemistry class and in the lab, converting between molar concentration (mol/L) and mass concentration (g/L) via the molar mass is one of the most fundamental calculations.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the molar mass of a compound?

The molar mass is the sum of the atomic masses of all atoms in the chemical formula, e.g. NaCl = 22.990 (Na) + 35.45 (Cl) = 58.44 g/mol.

What is the difference between mol/L and mg/L?

mol/L (molar concentration) counts particles regardless of their mass, while mg/L (mass concentration) measures weight per liter. The conversion uses the molar mass: mass = amount of substance × molar mass.

What does ppm mean for water quality values?

ppm (parts per million) is approximately equal to mg/L for dilute aqueous solutions, since the density of water is close to 1 kg/L. This approximation no longer holds for other solvents or high concentrations.

How do you calculate the molar mass of a hydrate like MgSO4·7H2O?

You add the molar mass of the base compound (MgSO₄) to the molar mass of the water of crystallization, multiplied by the number of water molecules (here 7 × 18.015 g/mol for 7 H₂O).

Does an ion's charge (e.g. SO4²⁻) change the molar mass?

Practically not: the mass of an electron is negligible compared to the total mass of an ion, so the molar mass of ions is calculated the same way, from the sum of the atomic masses.

What is the difference between molarity and molality?

Molarity (mol/L) relates the amount of substance to the volume of the finished solution, while molality (mol/kg) relates it to the mass of the pure solvent. Molality is temperature-independent (mass doesn't change with temperature), whereas molarity is slightly temperature-dependent, since liquid volume changes with temperature. Converting between the two additionally requires the density of the solution.
