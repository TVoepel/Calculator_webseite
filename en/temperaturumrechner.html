Formulas

Celsius → Fahrenheit: (°C × 9/5) + 32 Fahrenheit → Celsius: (°F − 32) × 5/9 Celsius → Kelvin: °C + 273.15 Kelvin → Celsius: K − 273.15 Kelvin → Rankine: K × 9/5 Rankine → Kelvin: °R × 5/9 Celsius → Réaumur: °C × 4/5 Réaumur → Celsius: °Ré × 5/4

Kelvin is the SI base unit of temperature; all other scales are converted internally via Kelvin to avoid rounding errors.

Sources: BIPM – SI Brochure (Kelvin definition), NIST – Guide to Unit Conversion, Wikipedia – Celsius

All supported temperature units

With this temperature converter you can directly convert Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F), Kelvin (K), Rankine (°R), and Réaumur (°Ré) into one another.

0 °C corresponds to 32 °F and 273.15 K – the freezing point of water. 100 °C corresponds to 212 °F and 373.15 K – the boiling point of water at standard pressure.

In practice: when you need to convert temperatures

US baking recipes and oven displays use Fahrenheit instead of Celsius – 350 °F (a very common value in US recipes) corresponds to about 177 °C.

Weather reports in the US are given in Fahrenheit; if you're planning a trip, you'll want a quick sense of how warm or cold a forecast value actually feels.

In physics and engineering, Kelvin is often used, for example for the color temperature of lamps or in scientific calculations where absolute zero is relevant.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Formula: °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32. Example: 20 °C corresponds to (20 × 9/5) + 32 = 68 °F.

How many degrees Celsius is 0 Kelvin?

0 Kelvin is absolute zero and corresponds to −273.15 °C – the theoretical lowest possible temperature.

Why does the US use Fahrenheit instead of Celsius?

The Fahrenheit scale was developed in 1724 by Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit and became established in the US historically, while most other countries switched to Celsius (part of the metric system) during the 20th century.

What is normal body temperature in Fahrenheit?

The commonly cited "normal" body temperature of 37 °C corresponds to 98.6 °F; modern studies, however, show a certain amount of individual variation.

What is the difference between Celsius, Kelvin, and Rankine?

Celsius and Kelvin have the same scale division, just with a shifted zero point (0 °C = 273.15 K). Rankine is the Fahrenheit counterpart to Kelvin: 0 °R likewise corresponds to absolute zero, but uses Fahrenheit-sized degrees.
