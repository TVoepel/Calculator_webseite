Formula

Result = Value × Factor(from) ÷ Factor(to), base unit = 1 cubic meter (m³)

Example: 1 liter equals 0.001 cubic meters. That means kitchen, lab, and technical volumes can all be converted with the same logic.

Sources: BIPM – SI Brochure (liter as an accepted unit), NIST – Guide to Unit Conversion, Wikipedia – Liter

All supported volume units

With this volume converter you can directly convert liters, milliliters, microliters, cubic meters, cubic centimeters, cubic millimeters, US gallons, UK gallons, cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, fluid ounces, cubic feet, cubic inches, and oil barrels.

US measures (cup, tablespoon, teaspoon, gallon) are based on US kitchen and trade measures; UK measures follow the British imperial system (4.54609 liters per gallon).

In practice: when you need to convert volumes

American baking recipes usually measure ingredients in cups, tablespoons, and teaspoons instead of milliliters or grams – without conversion, the wrong amounts can quickly end up in the batter.

US gallons and UK gallons differ (3.785 L vs. 4.546 L), which matters when comparing fuel consumption, tank sizes, or oil barrels.

In aquariums and labs, people also switch between liters, milliliters, and microliters, for example when dosing water conditioners or reagents.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many ml is a US cup?

A US cup (cooking measure) equals 236.588 ml – not to be confused with the "legal cup" (240 ml), which is used in the US only for nutrition labeling.

How many liters is a US gallon?

1 US gallon (liquid gallon) equals exactly 3.785411784 liters. The British (imperial) gallon is notably larger at 4.54609 liters.

How many ml are a tablespoon and a teaspoon?

A US tablespoon holds 14.79 ml, a US teaspoon 4.93 ml – a factor of 3 between the two.

How many liters does an oil barrel hold?

An oil barrel equals 158.987 liters (42 US gallons) – the internationally standard trading unit for crude oil.

Why do US and UK measurements differ in cooking?

The US still uses the US customary system today, while the UK historically used the imperial system – both trace back to different historical standardizations and were never fully unified.
